# Vision — what is being built, and for whom

**As of:** 2026-09-18 · **Owner:** Dan Beseda · Source: Dan's brief of 2026-09-18.

This is the north star for the chief agent and every builder. When a backlog
item is ambiguous, resolve it against this file. When this file is wrong, Dan
changes it — builders do not.

## The one-sentence version

A complete, working company brain for a fictional adaptogenic coffee brand,
built in public by a chief agent and its builders, one piece at a time, so that
anyone can click through how an AI-run operation actually works.

## Who looks at it, and what they must come away believing

The visitor is a founder, COO or hiring manager deciding whether Dan can build
this for them. After five minutes they must believe three things:

1. **It is real work, not a diagram.** Skills have runbooks, reports have
   numbers, the numbers agree with each other across departments.
2. **It is a system, not a folder of prompts.** Conventions, governance, dated
   knowledge, decisions, a staleness check, PII rules.
3. **It grows.** The build log shows agents adding to it week after week.

## The visitor's path

1. **Dan's resume** (separate site). One click to →
2. **The brain explorer.** A map of the company: departments as clickable
   areas. Each department shows its agents, its skills, its daily and weekly
   routines, and its reports.
3. **How it works.** A diagram: knowledge in Markdown in GitHub, agents on top,
   connectors out to the tools the company already pays for. A list of
   connectors: what each one pulls, which department uses it.
4. **The brain itself.** Browse the actual files — company, products,
   operations, marketing, decisions — rendered, navigable.
5. **Reports.** Every department has live-looking reports generated from the
   simulated data.
6. **Ask the brain.** A chat where visitors ask what's in the brain, how it
   works, and *why it was built this way*. (Needs a backend — see backlog phase 8.)
7. **The build log.** How the chief agent built all of this, entry by entry.

## The company

A small DTC brand selling adaptogenic coffee, based in Prague, selling across
the EU. Shopify storefront, a Czech 3PL, subscriptions, a creator programme.
Everything about it is fictional. Its canonical facts live in
`company/facts.md` once phase 0 is done.

Catalogue shape (fixed by Dan): **four SKUs, one bundle, one starter kit.** The
starter kit is kitted — it has a bill of materials and needs a kitting work
order at the 3PL.

## Departments and what each must contain

### Brand
- Name, story, positioning, voice, visual identity, brand manual as a skill
- A storefront website for the fictional brand, designed by the brand builder

### Operations
- Skills: pull orders (Shopify-shaped), check orders are healthy, check the 3PL
  received and shipped them, stock levels, kitting work orders
- Customer service: ticket pull, drafted replies, a support playbook
- Reports: **tickets** (volume, themes, anything wrong), **orders in and out**
  (flowing smoothly or not), **stock** — per SKU, with supplier lead times,
  weeks of cover, and an **alert on any week projected below safety stock**

### Marketing
- Structure mirrors how Parker structures marketing (Dan will supply the map)
- A marketing lead who builds the department and the agents under it
- **Daily competitor research** report
- **Metrics overview**: CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC, MER, AOV, repeat rate, subscription churn
- **Creator pipeline**: every creator, their stage, and where they came from
  (source)

### Marketing operations
- The machinery behind marketing: calendars, briefs, tracking, attribution hygiene

### Founder desk
- Three drafting runs a day — **morning, midday, end of day** — drafting replies
  to the founder's emails
- Shows the **draft next to what the founder actually sent**, and what the
  system learned from the difference (a style-rules file that visibly improves)

### The brain's intake
- How knowledge gets in: **Google Meet transcripts, Slack, email**, the brand
  manual, surveys, reviews
- Shows the collection → redaction → corpus → analysis flow

## Principles

- **Data is generated by code, never typed by an agent.** One seeded simulation
  produces orders, shipments, stock, tickets, marketing spend and creators. Every
  report is computed from it. That is what makes the numbers agree.
- **Everything fictional.** No real customer, no real person's name, no real
  company presented as a partner. Real competitors may appear in *competitor
  research* only, described from public information.
- **No health claims.** EU law allows no approved claims for functional
  mushrooms. The brand says what is in it, not what it does to you. The
  compliance reviewer enforces this.
- **Build slowly and finish things.** One backlog item per session, done to its
  definition of done, before the next begins.
